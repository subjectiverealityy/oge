Fronet 1

Designed and developed the frontend of a web app that would display real-time information to logged-in financiers about their revenue, anonymised user transaction data, internet usage data, and network performance data from the hostel WiFi deployments they helped fund.

Using PDF reports of hostels that were provided to me as a basis for the data that would be displayed, I created a low-fidelity Figma wireframe of the proposed visual structure of the application. After the founder approved it, I designed it using Claude.ai's artifact feature, and iterated on the design until the founder approved its development. I then copied the artifact's code into Visual Studio Code, and revised it before deploying the web app for user testing.

The app was shown to potential users and I made iterations based on their feedback. The current stage is integration of the endpoints that the backend developers provided recently. I work at the ISP (Internet Service Provider) as their sole frontend developer and designer. I also helped the founder turn a landing page that he generated in Claude, into a responsive, deployed one, with its own codebase.

Fronet 2

I built a captive portal for Fronet Wireless Ltd. A captive portal is a web page that appears when you connect to a Wi-Fi network (it asks you to complete an action before you can access the internet - in this case, their internet subscribers used it to access the WiFi they had paid for by inputting a voucher code that had been sent to them). Recently, I added Google Analytics tracking to the captive portal and tested it across different devices and browsers.

I downloaded the codebase template provided by the company's network platform then updated the captive portal to add the company's branding, a 30-second advertisement, an external link to the company's website an external link to one of their subscription plans, and a terms of service checkbox and modal that users must agree to before they can send their voucher code and connect to the WiFi.

It was a legacy codebase written in HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and jQuery. Interestingly, there was a comment in Chinese in one of the files. I found that updating some parts of the codebase would disrupt the webpage's connection to the internet controller hardware so I did research to find out the parts I could safely update and commented out unused code rather than deleting it (in case it was needed in a future development session)
